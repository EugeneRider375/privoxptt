THE PROBLEM

People are spread out, decisions cannot wait

On any working property several services operate at once, scattered across the grounds: a guard at the gate, an engineer in the plant room, housekeeping on the floors, the manager in the office.

A phone call does not solve this — you need the number, you wait for an answer, then you repeat yourself to the second and third person. A handheld radio works on one site but runs out of range and never links two properties together.

WHAT GETS IN THE WAY

Range

Analogue radio stops at the property line. Between two sites there is simply no link.

WHAT GETS IN THE WAY

Fragmentation

Every site has its own radios and its own frequencies. Management has no shared picture.

WHAT GETS IN THE WAY

Nights and weekends

With nobody at the desk, an incident is discovered in the morning — by its consequences.

THE SYSTEM

Push-to-talk over cellular and Wi-Fi

PRIVOX is press-and-speak communication carried over ordinary internet. Your voice reaches the whole group instantly — no number to dial, no waiting for anyone to pick up.

Distance stops being a factor: people in one group can be on different properties, in different cities and countries, and still hear each other as if they stood together.

The server can sit on your side. That is what separates PRIVOX from cloud radio services: conversations and data stay inside your perimeter, and the system does not depend on somebody else's subscription or somebody else's decisions. It can also be hosted by us — the choice is yours.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR A CONTRACTOR

Many properties, one system

A security or facility company does not serve one address — it serves a dozen: hotels, villa estates, construction sites, residential complexes. Usually each has its own radios, its own frequencies and no shared view at all.

In PRIVOX the properties live in one system without interfering with each other: each has its own groups and its own people, while management sees everything at once. A shift on one site does not hear the traffic of another — unless you deliberately create a shared channel for supervisors.

WHAT SETS IT APART

The property joins the channel too

Besides people, sensors connect to the system: cold room temperature, leaks and flooding, the condition of technical rooms. An alarm lands in the same channel where the shift works, and is mirrored to a manager in Telegram.

A sensor connects without modifying the hardware — the server polls it through its normal interface. Each has its own limits: one set for a freezer, another for a plant room.

THRESHOLDS

Its own normal for each

A minimum, a maximum or both. Crossing the line raises the alarm immediately.

NO NOISE

One alarm, not two hundred

The signal fires at the moment a value crosses the threshold, not every fifteen seconds while the problem lasts.

THE IMPORTANT ONE

Silence is an alarm too

A sensor that stopped answering is more dangerous than one reporting bad numbers: a cold room may have been thawing for an hour while the screen still shows the last good temperature.

CONSOLE

The whole picture on one screen

The duty operator sees the channels of every site, the shift roster with who is reachable right now, the sensors and their state — and can speak into any group without joining it.

The dispatcher console. Channels on the left, then the site's sensors — one in alarm, two silent. On the right the shift: who is on air, with a call button for each person.

The shift on the map. Markers move with the people; the tooltip carries the callsign and speed. You can see who is closest to an incident.

DEVICES

Phones and radios on the same channel

A guard with a radio, a technician with a phone and a dispatcher at a desk sit in the same group and hear each other. No gateways, no adapters.

PHONE

An app for Android and iPhone. An incoming call wakes a phone in a pocket — even with the app closed.

RADIO

A hardware talk key, one-handed operation, works with gloves on. For the posts where a phone is awkward.

Which devices suit you is a conversation, not a package. In some places the phones people already carry are enough; at fixed posts a proper radio earns its keep. We match this to the property rather than selling one standard kit.
