01 · VOICE

Four ways to reach someone

An ordinary radio does one thing: it talks to a channel. Real work needs more — sometimes one person, sometimes the entire shift, and sometimes at three in the morning.

GROUP CHANNEL

Hold the button, talk

Classic push-to-talk. Hold the key and the whole group hears you. One speaker holds the channel; everyone else sees who is on air.

That discipline is the point, not a limitation — unlike a conference call, three people never talk at once.

PRIVATE CALL

Full duplex, one to one

A private conversation with one person, exactly like a phone call — both sides talk at the same time.

The rest of the group neither hears it nor loses the channel.

GROUP ALERT

Get everyone, now

One button, and every member's phone rings for real — in a pocket, in a bag, on a night table, even with the app closed.

They answer and land straight on the channel.

EMERGENCY

SOS and dispatcher call

A separate panic button reaches the whole organisation, crossing group boundaries. A dispatcher call goes to whoever is on duty today.

Nothing suppresses SOS — not a schedule, not a group setting.

Distance is irrelevant. This is not a radio set with a coverage radius: everything runs through a server over ordinary internet. People in one group can sit in different cities and countries — on site, on a business trip, on another continent — and hear each other as if they stood together. All it needs is a connection: wired, Wi-Fi or cellular.

You can see who is reachable before you try. The system tells three states apart, so nobody wastes time calling a phone that is switched off.

03 · SENSORS AND ALARMS

The site joins the channel

This is the part no ordinary radio does. A sensor is a subscriber like any other: when something goes wrong, it raises the alarm in the same channel where the shift is working.

CONNECTION

No hardware changes

The server polls the sensor over its normal interface. No firmware work — the equipment stays exactly as it is.

A device can also push readings itself, if that suits it better.

THRESHOLDS

Its own normal for each

Every sensor carries its own limits: a fridge between 2 and 8 degrees, a warehouse below 70% humidity.

Set a minimum, a maximum, or both.

NO SPAM

One alarm, once

The alarm fires when a value crosses the line, not every fifteen seconds for as long as the problem lasts.

Otherwise people get used to alarms and stop looking at them.

Three sensor states the dispatcher sees:

NORMAL— within limits ALARM— threshold crossed SILENT— no data for too long

"Silent" is an alarm of its own, and that matters. A sensor that stopped answering is more dangerous than one reporting bad numbers: a freezer may have been thawing for an hour while the screen still shows the last good temperature. The system treats missing data as reason enough to get people moving.

04 · DISPATCHER CONSOLE

Everything on one screen

The console is where the rest comes together: people, site and conversation. The dispatcher never switches tabs to understand the situation.

LIVE MAP

Where the shift is

Subscriber markers update in real time as people move. The tooltip shows the callsign and current speed.

Speed matters more than it sounds: you can see whether a vehicle is driving or has been parked for twenty minutes. Sending the nearest person becomes a glance, not a round of phone calls.

CONSOLE

Groups, people and sensors side by side

Channels on the left, the shift roster with reachability on the right, sensors in their own panel. An alarm turns red right there, without taking you off the screen.

The dispatcher can talk into any group without joining it.

LOG

Who was on air, and when

Sign-ons and sign-offs are recorded. Reviewing a shift afterwards — who was reachable when an incident happened — works the next day just as well.

MESSAGES

Chats in the same window

Group and private messages open next to the live channel. An address, a plate number or a photo of a fault does not get lost in a stream of voice.

The map is a dispatch tool, not surveillance. Location sharing can be switched off for an individual — a contractor, or a driver in a private car. That permission is granted separately from the right to talk and to write.

05 · CONTROL

Who, where, and until when

Communication on a site is not only about being heard. It is also about who may speak, who sees other people's positions, and what happens to the access of someone who left last month.

ROLES

Four levels of access

A subscriber talks in their own groups. A dispatcher hears all of them and can talk into any. An administrator creates people and groups. The owner sees the whole system.

VALIDITY

A group for the duration of the job

A group can carry a start and end date. A shift, an event, a one-month contract — the channel closes itself when the time is up.

History stays readable: reviewing a shift after it ends is still possible.

PERMISSIONS

Different roles inside one group

A member can be allowed to listen but not speak, to write but not share a position. An observer, a trainee, a contractor — each gets what fits.

ACCOUNTS

Access with an expiry date

A seasonal worker gets access until a set date, and it lapses on its own. A lost device is cut off in one action, active sessions included.

AUDIT

Who changed what

Administrator actions — creating people, issuing and revoking invitations — are recorded separately from the live channel.

MESSAGING

Chat where the voice is

Group and private chats, history on the server, photos and documents up to 10 MB. You cannot read out a serial number over the air.

06 · WHO NEEDS THIS

Where it pays for itself

These situations share one thing: people need to reach each other fast, and the site needs to reach people fast. The more a minute of downtime or a missed alarm costs, the more obvious the return.

SECURITY

Guarding companies, in-house security

A shift on the ground, patrols, fixed posts. They need instant contact and a panic button you can find without looking.

  • SOS from any device, reaching the whole organisation
  • The dispatcher sees on the map who is nearest

RETAIL AND FOOD SERVICE

Store chains, restaurants, kitchens

Here the sensors pay off faster than the radio: one thawed cold room costs more than a year of running the system.

  • Cold room monitoring with alarms on any excursion
  • A night alarm wakes the person responsible instead of waiting for morning
  • Floor and kitchen staff on one channel

CONSTRUCTION AND INDUSTRY

Sites, workshops, warehouses

Noise, gloves, hard hats. You need a physical key and communication that works across the site without a conversation about tariffs.

  • Radios with a real button for people in the field
  • Site managers and the office on the same channel, from phones
  • Groups scoped to a contract close themselves

FACILITIES AND UTILITIES

Property management, maintenance

Emergency crews, addresses across town, night duty. Sensors in basements and server rooms warn you before the tenants call.

Fleets, warehouses, delivery

EVENTS
